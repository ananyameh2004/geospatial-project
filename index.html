Where was she,
and when?

In March 2022, Prof. Wirfs-Brock visited Whitman College for a job interview. Her phone logged a GPS point every minute. This is what those 1,618 pings reveal.

CS / Data Journalism · Whitman College · Dataset: prof-W-B-location-edited.csv

The Data

The dataset spans four days March 9 through 12, 2022 and covers Prof. Wirfs-Brock's entire trip from arrival to departure. The coordinates range from Walla Walla all the way to the Denver area, where she was mid-flight on her way home.

1,618 GPS points
4 Days tracked
812 mi Furthest from campus

I filtered the data to a Walla Walla bounding box (roughly lat 45.9-46.2, lon −118.5 to −118.1) to separate the 1,171 local points from the 447 travel points logged while she was in the air.

Part A Distance from a Chosen Location

I picked Marcus Whitman Hotel (lat 46.0676, lon -118.3437) as my reference point, found by right-clicking in Google Maps. Using the Haversine formula, I calculated the distance from every GPS point to this location.

Haversine formula computes the great-circle distance between two lat/lon coordinates, accounting for Earth's curvature.

a = sin²(Δlat/2) + cos(lat₁)·cos(lat₂)·sin²(Δlon/2)
d = 2R · arctan2(√a, √(1−a)), where R = 3,958.8 miles
Closest: 0.0459 miles (243 feet) at 2:10 AM on March 11, at coords 46.06795, -118.34288.
Furthest in Walla Walla: 3.88 miles at 3:15 PM on March 9, at coords 46.08500, -118.26684.
Furthest overall: 812.39 miles at 1:34 PM on March 12, at coords 39.84251, -104.67573. She was mid-flight heading home.

Part B Hypothesis

Hypothesis: Prof. WB spent more time near Olin Hall during business hours (9am to 5pm) than in the evenings, reflecting a typical interview-day schedule where she would be on campus during the day and elsewhere at night.

To test this, I calculated the distance from every Walla Walla GPS point to Olin Hall (lat 46.0717, lon −118.3273) and grouped the results by hour of day.

Distance from Olin Hall by Hour

Fig. 2 Average distance from Olin Hall per hour of day (Pacific Time). Numbers on bars show GPS point count for that hour.

The chart shows that Prof. WB was closest to Olin Hall between 9am and 1pm, with average distances under 0.2 miles during those hours. Distance increases in the afternoon and stays elevated through the evening, consistent with leaving campus after a full day of interviews. The 5am bar is an outlier with only 39 data points, so that spike is not very reliable.

During business hours (9am to 5pm), Prof. WB averaged 0.565 miles from Olin Hall across 221 GPS points. In the evenings (6pm onward), that average rose to 0.729 miles across 595 points, a difference of 0.164 miles. The hypothesis is supported: she was measurably closer to campus during the day than at night.

Process

All analysis was done in Python using pandas and plotly in Google Colab. Timestamps were converted from UTC to Pacific Time before any hour-of-day grouping. The Haversine formula was implemented from scratch using Python's math module.

One thing that surprised me was how clearly the hourly distance chart reflected a real schedule. I also had to be careful about timezones since the raw data was in UTC and converting to Pacific Time changed which hours the data fell into.
